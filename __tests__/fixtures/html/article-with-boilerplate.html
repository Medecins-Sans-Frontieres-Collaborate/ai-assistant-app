Floods displace thousands across Jonglei

Heavy seasonal rain has pushed the Nile beyond its banks for the third consecutive week, and floodwaters reached the outskirts of Bor on Tuesday morning. Local officials estimate that more than eleven thousand households have been forced from their homes since the start of the month, with the worst damage concentrated along the river.

Aid agencies operating in Jonglei State say the scale of displacement is now comparable to the 2021 emergency. Convoys carrying shelter kits and water purification tablets left Juba on Monday, though several roads remain impassable and deliveries have been delayed by days.

Conditions in the displacement sites

At the informal settlement outside Panyagor, families are sheltering on raised embankments with little protection from the rain. Health workers have recorded a rise in respiratory infections among young children, and clean water remains the most urgent gap according to the latest situation report.

Staff at the UNHCR field office in Bentiu confirmed that a secondary wave of arrivals is expected if the rains continue through the end of the month. Registration teams have been redeployed from Malakal to help manage the intake.

The national meteorological service has forecast a further two weeks of above-average rainfall across the region. Engineers are working to reinforce the dyke system north of Bor, but officials acknowledge that the existing defences were not built for water levels of this height.

Humanitarian coordinators meeting in Juba on Wednesday requested additional funding for the response, warning that current stocks of emergency shelter material will be exhausted within ten days.
